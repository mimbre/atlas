# Atlas: vision, mission and aims

## Vision

A world where AI amplifies human intelligence in software development.

## Mission

In search of human intelligence: finding where people are irreplaceable, and handing everything else to machinery.

Using Atlas means being empowered by AI. Building Atlas means searching for where human intelligence is needed. That boundary is not fixed; it shifts as evidence accumulates.

## Aims

1. **No supervising.** People never watch agents to keep work moving.
2. **Clear authority.** Agents never hold the keys or approve their own work.
3. **Verifiable results.** Accepted work has an independent review and proof.
4. **The line moves on evidence.** Every human intervention is recorded with its reason. It becomes a rule or agent work when it can, and goes back to people when automation fails.

## Principles

A principle holds for anyone, in any case. The system enforces it; instructions alone are not enough. There are only a few. Principles live in the decision ledger as decisions of force `principle`, each with what enforces it.

**AI may decide; a decider gate guards when.**
For each decision, a decider gate determines whether a person is needed. If not, AI decides, and the decision is recorded and reviewable. If a person is needed and takes too long, AI decides anyway so work does not stall. That decision gets a red flag, and work built on it is marked "based on unverified decisions" until a person has reviewed it; if the person overrules it, that work is flagged for review. Amending a principle, and a production release while rollback is not automated, always wait for a person.

**No worker agent assigns work.**
A worker never assigns work, to itself or to another worker. People, Flow, or an agent whose role is to assign do, visibly and reviewably.

**Workers hold no credentials.**
A worker never holds keys or logins for model providers or other services.

**No agent accepts its own work.**
Work counts as done only after an independent review and proof, bound to the exact version reviewed.

**Production needs a person's OK, as long as rollback is not automated.**
Production is where mistakes reach users. Until rollback is automated, a person decides whether a release goes out.

**Every open item has exactly one owner.**
Waiting is not a status: the item moves to whoever it waits for.

### Beliefs that guide direction

These guide where Atlas goes. They are not unbreakable, so they are not principles.

**Machinery is layered.** Code and rules hold human intelligence that people have understood well enough to write down. Agents handle work that can be described but not yet fully specified, steered by human-written instructions. People handle what is not yet captured. As understanding grows, work moves from people to agents to code; when a layer keeps failing, it moves back up.

**Every product materializes its own intelligence.** Every product built with Atlas follows the same process: human intelligence is gradually captured, first as agent instructions, then as code and rules. Every lesson, such as an incident, a review finding or a repeated decision, improves that product's own machinery. Atlas is the first such product: Atlas builds Atlas, and the process is proven there before others depend on it.

## Premise

**Every serious app becomes a service that does ongoing work for its users, with agents.** A standalone app is not enough. Agentic support belongs in every app's core, not added on later as a feature. That core includes Flows with gates, so agents cannot go haywire: Flows decide what may happen next, and at points that need judgment a gate advises. Rules on top of that advice make the decision. Fine-tuning these gates, both their questions and the rules on top, is part of developing the product, like any other change. Such a service needs what Atlas provides: supervised agents, durable facts, work that keeps moving, owners and decisions. Every product built with Atlas therefore shares the Atlas architecture, with its own flows, entities, connectors and clients. Who hosts it is decided per product.

## Unique selling point

**Machinery that improves itself, fed by human intelligence.**

Other tools improve when their vendor improves the model or harness, and the products built with them keep nothing. With Atlas, every lesson from building a product, such as an incident, a review finding or a repeated decision, becomes that product's own machinery: agent instructions first, then code and rules. Each improvement is normal work, proposed, reviewed and accepted. The machinery grows only from what people contribute and approve, and it stays with the product even if the AI vendor changes.
